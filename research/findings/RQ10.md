# RQ10: What do synthetic twin calls tell us? (out-of-sample test of `facts`)

**Answer in one sentence:** on 74 freshly written twin scripts, `facts` keeps its false-alarm advantage over the free
judge (FAR 14% vs 61%, real) but misses more (33% vs 4%, real) and no longer ranks calls better (flag AUC 0.82 vs
0.83). The tuned policy table loses more from dev to synthetic than the blind one (-0.08 vs -0.02 AUC), so some
dev-fitting is confirmed, but the tuned table still edges the blind one (0.82 vs 0.76). (confidence: medium-low. The
text is synthetic, written in the same style and **mostly by the same model that judges it**, and there are only 26
scenario units.)

Synthetic calls were used **only as a test set**. Their use as few-shot pools, training data for learned fusion, or
prompt-development data (the other half of RQ10) was **not measured**.

## Setup
- experiments/synth_stress.yaml (74 scripts) and synth_stress_audited.yaml (65), commit `1fd19e7` (dirty), profile
  `synth-oracle`, split `external` (no fold of config/splits.yaml: no dev or holdout call involved).
- **Scenarios**: 26, in config/synth_scenarios.yaml, written fresh. They use other companies, relatives, amounts and
  speaker order, include misconduct by the *advisor*, and include training quotes. Only config/criteria.yaml is shared
  with the case. The fact-question wording was deliberately **not** reused.
- **Twins**: each scenario has an alarm, a harmless and a review variant that share the setting and differ in the
  decisive fact. 30 scripts were written freely and 44 are minimal-pair rewrites of their base. Labels come from the
  scenario file, never from the LLM: 25 alarm, 28 no_alert, 21 review.
- Language: Swiss Standard German. The generator's dialect was broken pseudo-dialect with loops, so it was dropped.
  Text only (ORACLE: no audio, no ASR).
- **Label audit before any result**: a different model family (Claude) read every script, marking 4 invalid and 5
  doubtful. The audited subset is 65.
- Judge: Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-AWQ. CV folds and bootstrap unit = scenario (26 units). Bootstrap 2000x.

### Correction on circularity (important)
The journal (entries 04:08 and 04:15) says the scripts were written by Qwen3-8B, "a different model than the judge".
The data says otherwise. `data_synth/v1/manifest.json` records **Qwen/Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-AWQ as the generator for 71
of 74 scripts** (Qwen3-8B for 3), and the experiment spec says "the scripts are written by the same served LLM that
judges them". So for 96% of the set, **the generator and the judge are the same model**. The safeguards that still
hold: labels from the scenario file; scenario wording independent of the fact questions; a blind audit by another
model family; and minimal-pair twins, so a label difference rests on one decisive fact. Same-model circularity most
likely flatters the *free judge*, which reads text in its own style. That fits v1 scoring far better here
(0.83-0.89) than on dev (0.68).

## Where facts misses (positives flagged, CV, all 74)
| family | v1 | facts (tuned) | facts (blind) |
|---|---|---|---|
| access-type (access + numbers twins) | 12/12 | **3/12** | 10/12 |
| disclosure | 7/9 | 6/9 | 4/9 |
| documentation | 8/8 | 6/8 | 8/8 |
| splitting | 8/8 | 8/8 | **2/8** |
| trade | 9/9 | 8/9 | 8/9 |

Systematic causes (journal 04:15): `current` comes back "unknown" for every read-out code, even "noch zehn Minuten
gültig", and no rule covers spoken = yes & current = unclear. Trade `inside_info` examples list only family/employer,
so an *advisor* as the inside source goes to review. Unsolicited advisor disclosure goes to review (no "you have no
power of attorney" sentence). A leading advisor question ("um den Finanzchef zu umgehen, richtig?") counts as
avoid_control = yes although the customer denies it.

## Calls that flip (v1 -> facts, 74, CV): 42 calls
Broken, among others: four clear access alarms (Synth_access_001, 004, 007, 010: alarm -> no_alert) and
Synth_numbers_006. Fixed, among others: harmless Synth_trade_005 and Synth_trade_014 (v1 alarm 0.96 / 0.93 ->
no_alert) and Synth_access_005 (alarm -> no_alert).

## What it says about facts overfitting
1. The FAR advantage is **not** an artefact of dev wording: it holds out of sample and is real.
2. Some dev-fitting is real: the tuned table drops 0.08 AUC, the blind one 0.02. Combined with RQ5/RQ6, the honest
   range for unseen calls is AUC ~0.77-0.87 (the later paraphrase tests R3/R6 lower this to ~0.75-0.80).
3. The recall loss sits in a few rule gaps (access validity, advisor as the source), not spread evenly. A rule can fix
   these, but the first fix (table_v2, access.review_2) raised dev FAR to 36% (see RQ9).

## Recommendation
Keep facts with light keywords/patterns (the best balance here: cap met, AUC 0.86 / 0.92). Report the blind-table
number next to the tuned one as the honest range. Freeze this synthetic set as a **regression test** for every
policy-table change, and fix the access rules before claiming the access family.

## Strongest counter-argument
Synthetic scripts are more explicit than the case calls and have no ASR noise. They are written and judged by the
same Qwen model, and their 26 units give wide CIs. On this set judge + facts ranks best (0.90 / 0.94). If the hidden
calls resemble this set more than dev, the free judge should be back in the mix, at a FAR cost of over 50%.

## What would change our mind
A regenerated set from a non-Qwen generator that changes the ranking. Or holdout / hidden-set results where facts
misses alarms in families other than access.
