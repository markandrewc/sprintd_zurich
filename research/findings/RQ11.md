# RQ11: Does a LoRA fine-tune of Whisper on dev-fold case audio help?

**Answer in one sentence:** not tested tonight, on purpose. The ORACLE ceiling shows that perfect transcripts add only
about +0.03-0.05 flag AUC over the off-the-shelf large-v3 MIT model, so ASR is no longer the bottleneck. The night went
to the check design instead: fact questions vs the free judge moved AUC by +0.16 (real), and the full champion lands at
0.89 vs 0.68 for v1. (confidence: high that it was the right call for
the weekend; the fine-tune effect itself is **not measured**)

## Setup
- Queue item D2 ("Whisper LoRA fine-tune on dev-fold audio, evaluate on held-out fold", priority 5) stayed `todo`.
  No training run, no fold models, no numbers.
- Evidence for the decision: runs/experiments/llm_models (commit `1fd19e7`, dirty), dev 24 dialogues / 48 calls, same
  `facts` check and fact questions, only the input text varies.

## Why it was not run
| evidence | number | source |
|---|---|---|
| ORACLE ceiling (perfect dialect transcript), 14B | flag AUC 0.90 [0.79, 1.00] | llm_models `qwen25_14b_facts_oracle` |
| large-v3 MIT ASR, 14B | 0.85 [0.73, 0.96] | `qwen25_14b_facts_l3` |
| turbo ASR, 14B | 0.87 [0.76, 0.96] | `qwen25_14b_facts` |
| large-v3 MIT ASR, 7B (champion candidate) | 0.89 [0.77, 0.99] | `qwen25_7b_facts_l3` |
| ORACLE dialect transcript, 7B | 0.76 [0.56, 0.92] | `qwen25_7b_facts_oracle` |
| check design, same 7B (facts vs free judge) | +0.16 [+0.05, +0.28], real | RQ5 |

1. **ASR is not the bottleneck.** Even perfect words gain at most +0.03-0.05 AUC over large-v3 (14B), and nothing
   with the 7B. A fine-tune can only reach part of that ceiling. At n = 24 dialogues a gain of that size cannot be
   detected anyway.
2. **Time.** One overnight GPU (12 GB) shared by ASR sweeps, an LLM sweep and the synthetic test. Fine-tuning needs
   alignment of the scripts to audio, 5 fold models (the recording itself must never be trained on), conversion to
   CTranslate2, then ASR eval *and* detection per fold.
3. **Integrity risk.** Training on the case scripts and scoring the same calls would memorise them. That breaks the
   "no hidden hints" rule unless every number comes from a fold model that never saw the dialogue
   (docs/research/whisper_finetune_12gb.md §5).
4. **Little in-domain data.** 4.6 h of audio, 22 independent dialogue groups. The audio is TTS, so a fine-tune risks
   learning the TTS voice, not Swiss German callers.
5. Where ASR still fails is narrow: access words (K10 PIN/TAN/Passwort/Bestätigungscode) 14 of 18 spots found by
   large-v3 vs 9 of 18 by turbo (dev). The large-v3 model already fixed most of the gap.

## What docs/research/whisper_finetune_12gb.md proposes (desk research, nothing run)
- **Only worth training: a dialect-output ASR.** The Standard German view needs no fine-tune. A Standard German
  fine-tune is "probably skip" (expected gain ~2.8 WER points, gcoli's number over base turbo).
- **LoRA, not full fine-tune**, on `openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo` or `gcoli/whisper-large-v3-turbo-swiss-german-mit`
  (MIT): r = 32, alpha = 64, q_proj / v_proj, lr 3e-4, bf16, gradient checkpointing, batch 8 x accum 2, 2-3 epochs.
  A full turbo fine-tune with AdamW (~14.6 GB before activations) does not fit in 12 GB.
- **30 s windows with timestamp tokens** (50% timestamped, 50% with previous-text prompt), cut with i4ds
  `whisper-prep`. Training on padded sentences breaks segmentation and thus our snippet timestamps
  (arXiv 2412.15726). Check word timestamps against forced alignment afterwards.
- **Data**: `i4ds/swiss-german-city-sentences_v2` (dialect + Standard German text, licence unspecified), 20-40 h;
  our calls only via 5-fold GroupKFold (clean + noisy and twin pairs in the same fold). SDS-200 / STT4SG-350 need
  signed licences that won't arrive before Sunday.
- **Cost (estimate)**: 2-6 min per epoch on our 4.6 h; 15-40 min per epoch on 30 h packed; under 3 h in total.
- **Export**: merge the LoRA, copy generation_config.json (alignment heads), `ct2-transformers-converter ...
  --quantization float16`, profile with `condition_on_previous_text: false`.
- **No QLoRA of the LLM** for the submission: 30 labelled dialogues cannot separate a gain from noise.

## Recommendation
Do not fine-tune for Sunday. Ship gcoli large-v3 MIT as is. If there is time after the submission, the experiment
worth running is the dialect-output LoRA, judged first by the ORACLE question (does the dialect view win at all? See
RQ3: not with the 7B). Pitch line: "we measured that better transcripts would add at most a few AUC points, so we
invested in the decision logic instead".

## Strongest counter-argument
The case audio is synthetic. On real callers (accents, phone codecs, fast speech), ASR errors can be much larger than
here, and fine-tunes on real Swiss German speech are where published gains come from. Then the ORACLE gap would be
larger and a fine-tune worthwhile. Our ceiling argument holds only for audio like the case audio.

## What would change our mind
Real recordings where large-v3 keyword recall falls well below the 84% seen here, or an ORACLE-vs-ASR gap with a paired
CI that excludes 0 (more dialogues needed). Either would make a fold-wise LoRA run (D2) the next experiment.
